### Your Mission

You are joining the company with a long-term objective of becoming the **Business Development & Operations Manager**.

Your first three months are intentionally different from your final role.

For the first 90 days, you will work directly in:

* Sales
* Telesales
* Lead generation

* Proposal preparation

* Learning our products
* Learning our internal systems

During this period, you are expected to understand the business **from the customer's perspective and from the company's operational perspective**.

After the first three months, your responsibility will expand to:

# 1. The Most Important Principle

## You are expected to solve problems independently.

The company does not expect you to come to the founder/management for every small clarification.

Your responsibility is to:

1. Understand the problem.
2. Search the available company resources.
3. Check the CRM.
4. Check the product documentation.
5. Check previous similar cases.
6. Discuss with the responsible team member.
7. Identify possible solutions.
8. Make a reasonable decision.
9. Escalate only when the decision requires management authority.

### Do not approach management with:

> "What should I do?"

Instead approach with:

> "This is the problem. I checked A, B and C. I see two possible solutions. I recommend option A because ____. I need approval only for ____."

This is the expected management mindset.

You will naturally have questions during training.

* Training material
* Previous proposals
* Previous customer cases
* SOPs

## Level 3 — Discuss

If another employee owns the area, discuss it with them first.

## Level 4 — Recommend

If management input is required, come with a recommendation.

### Example

❌ Poor approach:

> "The client wants WhatsApp integration with their ecommerce system. I checked Grafty's current capabilities and the requirement appears possible. The client also needs X and Y, which may require development. I recommend selling Grafty + a custom integration package. I need technical confirmation on Y before committing to the client."

# 6. When You MUST Escalate

You should independently handle normal operational decisions.

However, certain decisions require management approval.

Escalate when there is:

### Financial risk

* Large discount
* Unusual pricing
* Refund
* Credit
* Waiver
* Major payment delay

### Contractual risk

* New contract terms
* Legal commitments
* Ownership/IP questions
* Major scope changes

### Technical risk

* Feature that does not currently exist
* Major custom development
* Uncertain integration
* Security concern
* Architecture-changing requirement

### Customer risk

* Serious complaint
* Threat of legal action
* Major refund demand
* Reputational issue
* High-value client escalation

### Strategic decision

* New product
* Major partnership
* New market
* Major hiring
* Significant expenditure

# 7. Your Decision-Making Framework

Before escalating, answer these five questions:

### 1. What happened?

Clearly describe the problem.

### 2. Why did it happen?

Identify the likely cause.

### 3. What have I already checked?

List your investigation.

### 4. What are the possible solutions?

Give at least two when possible.

### 5. What do I recommend?

Give your preferred solution and explain why.

Management should then be able to make a decision quickly.

# 27. Objection Handling

Common objections will include:

### "It's expensive."

Don't immediately discount.

Understand:

> "Compared to what?"

Then explain value.

---

### "Send me details."

Ask:

> "Certainly. Which part would you like me to include so I can send you the most relevant information?"

Continue the conversation.

---

### "I'll think about it."

Ask:

> "Absolutely. Is there anything specific you need to evaluate before making the decision?"

Find the actual objection.

---

### "We already use another system."

Ask:

> "What are you happy with in your current system, and where do you feel it is lacking?"

Then position accordingly.

# 37. Customer Escalation

When a customer complains, don't immediately forward the complaint to management.

First:

1. Understand the issue.
2. Check the customer account.
3. Check the agreed scope.
4. Check payment status.
5. Check previous communication.
6. Identify the cause.
7. Determine whether it is:

   * Training issue
   * Support issue
   * Product issue
   * Development issue
   * Scope issue
   * Customer misunderstanding
8. Propose a solution.
9. Escalate only if necessary.
