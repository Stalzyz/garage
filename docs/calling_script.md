# Grekam — Sales Calling Script
### Version 1.0 | Sales Team Reference | August 2026

---

> This is not a script to read from.
> It is a framework to internalize.
> Practice until it sounds natural.
> Then adapt it to your voice.

---

# BEFORE EVERY CALL

Open Grekam OS.
Check the lead record.
Read the previous notes.
Know the customer's name, company, and last interaction before you dial.

**Never call cold from a blank record.**
If you have no information, spend 2 minutes researching before you call.

---

# CALL TYPE 1 — FIRST COLD CALL
*For leads you are contacting for the first time.*

---

## Opening (First 20 seconds)

> "Hi, am I speaking with [Name]?"
>
> *(Wait for confirmation)*
>
> "Hi [Name], I'm [Your Name] from Grekam. We work with businesses on digital solutions — things like WhatsApp automation, ecommerce platforms, and custom software. I'm reaching out because I thought what we do might be relevant to your business. Do you have about 2 minutes?"

**If yes — continue.**
**If no:** *"No problem at all. When would be a better time for me to call back — tomorrow morning or afternoon?"*

Log a follow-up task immediately.

---

## Establishing Relevance (Next 30 seconds)

Do not pitch yet. Ask first.

> "Just so I don't waste your time — can I ask, how are you currently handling [relevant area based on their industry]?"

Examples by industry:
- Restaurant: "...how you're currently taking enquiries and orders?"
- Retail: "...how customers are reaching out to your store right now?"
- Services: "...how your team is following up with leads currently?"

**Listen. Do not interrupt.**

---

## Identifying the Problem (1 to 2 minutes)

Follow their answer with one of these:

> "That's interesting — and are you finding that process works well, or is there anything that feels like it's slipping through the cracks?"

or:

> "I see — and how many enquiries would you say you're getting per day roughly?"

or:

> "And right now, who's managing that on your end — is it just yourself or do you have a team?"

**You are listening for:**
- Volume of enquiries or transactions
- Current tool or process
- Number of people managing it
- Pain point or frustration

---

## The 30-Second Pitch (Only After Identifying a Problem)

Match your pitch to their specific pain. Do not use a generic pitch.

**If they mention manual WhatsApp work:**
> "That's exactly the kind of problem we solve with Grafty — it's our WhatsApp automation platform. Instead of your team manually responding to every message, Grafty handles the routine communication automatically. Your staff focuses on the real conversations that need human attention."

**If they mention their website or online store:**
> "That sounds like a good fit for Atlas — it's our ecommerce platform. It handles everything from product listings to orders, payments, and customer management in one place."

**If they mention needing a website or digital presence:**
> "We build custom websites and web applications for businesses — from simple business sites to full ecommerce platforms with CRM integration."

---

## The Ask — Getting to the Next Step

Never try to close on a cold call.
The only goal is to earn a demo or a second conversation.

> "I'd love to show you exactly how this works — it takes about 15 to 20 minutes and I can walk you through it live. Would that be useful?"

**If yes:**
> "Great. What works for you — would [Day] morning or [Day] afternoon suit you better?"

Confirm the appointment. Log it in Grekam OS immediately. Send a WhatsApp confirmation within 5 minutes.

**If hesitant:**
> "Completely fair — let me send you a quick overview on WhatsApp so you can see what it's about first. Then you can decide if a call makes sense. Is that okay?"

---

## Call Endings — All Scenarios

**Booked a demo:**
> "Perfect. I'll send you a WhatsApp confirmation right now with the time. Looking forward to speaking with you, [Name]."

**Sending information first:**
> "I'll send that across in the next few minutes. And I'll follow up in a couple of days to see what you think. Does that work?"

**Not interested:**
> "Not a problem at all. Can I ask — is it more that the timing isn't right, or does it not seem relevant to your business right now?"
> *(Listen — sometimes this opens a real conversation)*
> "Understood. I'll drop you a message in case anything changes. Have a good day."

**No answer:**
> *Do not leave a voicemail on the first attempt.*
> Log: "No answer — first attempt. Try again [specific date and time]."
> Send a WhatsApp message instead (see WhatsApp Script).

---

## After the Call — Grekam OS Logging

Log within 10 minutes:

```
Interaction type: Call
Outcome: [Connected / No answer / Busy / Wrong number]
Notes: [What they said. What problem was identified. What was sent.]
Next action: [Specific task with date and time]
Lead status: Update if changed
```

# CALL TYPE 2 — WARM FOLLOW-UP CALL
*For leads you have spoken to at least once before.*

---

## Opening

> "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from Grekam. We spoke last [day/week] — how are you?"

*(Brief personal exchange — 20 seconds maximum)*

> "I'm calling because I wanted to follow up on what we discussed. Have you had a chance to think about it?"

---

## If They Say Yes, I Was Thinking About It

> "Great — what questions came up for you?"

**Listen first. Then address the question specifically.**

Never answer a question with a pitch. Answer the question, then ask:

> "Does that help clarify it?"

---

## If They Say No, I Haven't Had Time

> "Completely understandable — things get busy. Can I take 2 minutes now to give you a quick recap and then we can decide if a demo makes sense?"

If yes — give a 2-minute summary of the most relevant benefit for their business.

---

## Re-Qualifying the Opportunity

If the lead has been warm but uncommitted for more than 2 weeks:

> "[Name], I want to be respectful of your time. Can I ask honestly — is this something you're actively considering, or has the priority shifted for now?"

**If still interested:** Book a concrete next step.
**If priority shifted:** Mark as On Hold with a return date. Do not leave it as Active with no movement.

---

# CALL TYPE 3 — POST-DEMO FOLLOW-UP
*Call made within 24 hours of a product demo.*

---

## Opening

> "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] from Grekam. I just wanted to follow up after yesterday's demo. How are you feeling about what you saw?"

---

## Listening Phase

Give them space to speak. Do not jump in with features.

> "What part stood out most for you?"

or:

> "Was there anything that wasn't clear or that you'd like to see again?"

---

## Handling Post-Demo Hesitation

**"I need to discuss with my partner/team":**
> "Of course — completely makes sense. When do you expect to have that conversation? I can follow up after that."
> Set a specific follow-up date.

**"I liked it but I'm not sure about the price":**
> "I understand. Can I ask — is it the monthly cost overall, or is it more about whether the return justifies it for your current volume?"
> *(This helps you understand if it's a budget problem or a value problem)*

**"Let me think about it":**
> "Absolutely. Is there a specific concern you're working through, or is it more about timing?"
> *(Find the real reason)*

---

## Moving to Proposal

If they are positive:

> "[Name], based on what you shared, I think [Product + Plan] is the right fit for you. I'd like to put together a proper proposal so you have everything in writing — the features, the pricing, and the onboarding plan. Would that be useful?"

If yes — confirm email. Tell them when they will receive it. Log a task.

---

# CALL TYPE 4 — PROPOSAL FOLLOW-UP
*Call made 2 business days after a proposal is sent.*

---

## Opening

> "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from Grekam. I sent across the proposal on [day] — did you get a chance to look through it?"

---

## If They Have Reviewed It

> "Great — what are your thoughts? Is there anything you'd like me to walk you through?"

Listen. Address each concern specifically.

---

## If They Haven't Reviewed It

> "No worries. Is there a better time for you to look at it — today or tomorrow? I can also walk you through the key points in 5 minutes right now if that's easier."

---

## The Price Conversation

If they question the price:

> "Can I ask what you were expecting the investment to be?"

*(Listen)*

> "I see. So the gap is around [₹X]. Let me understand your situation better so I can see what makes the most sense — is your main concern the monthly commitment, or is it the total cost for the first three months?"

Do not immediately offer a discount.
First: explore whether scope can be adjusted.
Second: explore whether payment terms can help.
Third: only offer a discount with manager approval if genuinely necessary.

---

## Closing Language

> "[Name], I've worked with a few businesses similar to yours and the common experience is that the first month is when it pays for itself — once [specific benefit relevant to them]. Based on what you've told me, I think the right move is to start with [specific plan] and we can grow from there. Shall we proceed?"

---

# CALL TYPE 5 — CLOSING CALL
*When a deal is agreed and payment is pending.*

---

## Opening

> "Hi [Name], great news — I just wanted to confirm the next steps so we can get you started. You mentioned you'd be ready to proceed this week — is that still the plan?"

---

## Confirming Payment

> "Once the payment is received, our team begins onboarding within [X] business days. I'll personally be your point of contact through that process."

---

## Handling Payment Delay

> "I understand — things can get delayed. Is there anything on our end we can do to make the process smoother? Some clients prefer a link, some prefer a bank transfer — whichever works best for you."

---

## If the Deal Seems to Be Cooling

> "[Name], I want to be straightforward with you. We've had a good conversation and I genuinely believe this will work well for your business. Is there anything holding you back that we haven't discussed yet?"

*(Listen carefully — this is where real objections surface)*

---

# CALL TYPE 6 — RE-ENGAGEMENT CALL
*For leads that went cold (no contact for 30+ days).*

---

## Opening

> "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] from Grekam. We spoke a while back about [product/topic]. I know the timing wasn't quite right at that point — I wanted to check in and see how things are going."

---

## The Re-Qualifying Question

> "Has anything changed on your end since we last spoke? Any new projects or challenges you're working through?"

**Do not pitch immediately.** Let them update you first.

---

## If Conditions Have Changed

Re-engage as a fresh conversation. Apply the full qualification process again.

---

## If Still Not Ready

> "No problem at all. Should I check back in a month, or would a longer break make more sense?"

Set a specific date. Do not let the lead float.

---

# UNIVERSAL CALL RULES

1. **Know the name before you dial.** "Hi, is this the owner?" is a cold start that sets a poor tone.

2. **Never pitch before you understand the problem.** Every pitch must connect to something they told you.

3. **Ask one question at a time.** Multiple questions in one breath confuse the customer and feel like an interrogation.

4. **Silence is not empty.** After asking a question, wait. Do not fill silence with more talking.

5. **Never argue.** If they push back, acknowledge and ask a question:
   > "I understand. Can I ask what's driving that concern?"

6. **Never end a call without a next step.** Every call must close with:
   > "So the next step is [X]. I'll [send/call/confirm] by [specific time]. Does that work?"

7. **Log immediately.** Do not batch your call notes at end of day.
