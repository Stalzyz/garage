# Grekam — Customer Onboarding SOP
### Version 1.0 | Internal Operations Document | August 2026

---

> Onboarding is where the sale becomes real.
> A customer who bought the product is not yet a customer who uses it.
> A customer who uses it is not yet a customer who succeeds with it.
> Your job does not end at payment.

---

# THE ONBOARDING PRINCIPLE

The first 30 days after payment determine whether a customer stays for 3 years or cancels after 3 months.

Most customers do not cancel because the product is bad.
They cancel because:
- Nobody guided them through setup
- They got stuck and had no one to call
- They never used the key features
- They couldn't measure the value
- The experience felt like a transaction, not a partnership

Onboarding fixes all of these.

---

# UNIVERSAL ONBOARDING RULES

These apply regardless of product.

1. **The clock starts at payment, not at the customer's convenience.** Initiate onboarding within 24 hours of payment confirmation.
2. **One person owns the onboarding.** No customer should wonder who their contact is.
3. **Never leave a customer without a next step.** Every onboarding session ends with a confirmed next action.
4. **Log everything in Grekam OS.** Every call, session, message, and status update belongs in the customer record.
5. **Do not assume the customer knows how to use the product.** Even technical customers need guidance on your implementation.
6. **Proactively communicate.** Do not wait for the customer to ask "what's happening?"

---

# ONBOARDING STAGES (Universal)

Every product follows this flow, with product-specific detail below:


## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1 — Payment Confirmation (Day 0)

Immediately after payment:
- Send the Welcome Message (see template below)
- Create customer record in Grekam OS with:
  - Business name and contact
  - Plan purchased
  - Payment date
  - Assigned account manager
  - Onboarding start date
- Create an onboarding task checklist in Grekam OS linked to this customer

---

### Step 2 — Welcome Call (Day 1)

Purpose: Introduce the onboarding process, set expectations, collect required information.

**Information to collect:**
- Business WhatsApp number to be used
- Whether they have an existing Meta Business Manager account
- Whether their business is Meta-verified
- Names and roles of team members who will use Grafty
- Primary use cases (order updates / enquiries / support / broadcasts)
- Existing customer contact list (format and size)

**What to confirm:**
- Onboarding timeline
- Point of contact on their side
- First training session date and time

---

### Step 3 — Account Setup (Day 1–3)

Technical setup handled by the Grafty/Tech team:
- Register their WhatsApp number on the Business API
- Set up their Grafty account
- Create user accounts for their team members
- Configure basic automation responses

**Customer requirement during this phase:**
- Access to their Facebook/Meta Business Manager
- Domain email for business verification
- Phone number ownership verification

---

### Step 4 — Template Creation and Approval (Day 3–5)

Work with the customer to create their first message templates.

Common templates to create at onboarding:
- Order confirmation
- Delivery update
- Appointment reminder
- Welcome message for new customers
- Promotional broadcast (if applicable)

**Important:** Meta reviews and approves templates. This can take 24–72 hours. Set this expectation with the customer upfront.

---

### Step 5 — Training Session (Day 5–6)

Conduct a live training session (video call or in-person).

**Training agenda:**
1. Dashboard overview (15 min)
2. Inbox and conversation management (10 min)
3. Using templates (10 min)
4. Setting up automated replies (10 min)
5. Running a broadcast (10 min)
6. Adding and managing contacts (10 min)
7. Reading reports (5 min)
8. Q&A (15 min)

**End of session:**
- Confirm they can perform basic tasks independently
- Share training resources / documentation links
- Confirm go-live date
- Book a check-in call for Day 14

---

### Step 6 — Go-Live (Day 7)

On go-live day:
- Confirm all templates are approved
- Confirm all users can log in
- Send the first live message together (don't let them do it alone the first time)
- Confirm their emergency contact if something goes wrong

---

## Grafty Onboarding — Common Problems and Solutions

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Meta verification delayed | Business documents not ready | Prepare checklist before onboarding starts |
| Template rejected | Wrong category or policy violation | Review Meta template guidelines; resubmit with correct category |
| Team not using the dashboard | Training not absorbed | Schedule a second training session, shorter, focused on daily workflow |
| Customer sends broadcasts incorrectly | Skipped broadcast training | Record a short video of the broadcast process; share it |
| Customer not seeing value | Using only basic features | Review their use case and enable automation or broadcasts |

| Day 7 post-launch | Check-in call | Account Manager |
| Day 30 post-launch | Health check | Account Manager |

---

## Step-by-Step Process

### Step 1 — Project Brief Collection

After the welcome call, send the Project Brief Form covering:

**Business information:**
- Business name, industry, target audience
- Brand colours, fonts (or "open to suggestions")
- Logo file (high-resolution)

**Product catalogue:**
- Number of products
- Categories and subcategories
- Product images (format and quantity)
- Pricing structure (fixed / variable / with discounts)
- GST applicability

**Order management:**
- Payment methods required
- Delivery / shipping setup
- COD handling
- Return and refund policy

**Access requirements:**
- Who will manage the store daily?
- How many admin users?

**Content:**
- Who provides product descriptions? (Client / Grekam writes it)
- Any existing website content to carry over?

---

### Step 2 — Catalogue Upload

Two options:
- **Client uploads:** Provide them with the data template (CSV format). Train them on the import process.
- **Grekam uploads:** Agree on the scope and add it to the project cost if not already included.

Do not begin development without a confirmed catalogue or at least 80% of the product data.

---

### Step 3 — UAT (User Acceptance Testing)

Before go-live, the customer must test:
- [ ] Browse products and search
- [ ] Add to cart and checkout
- [ ] Place a test order with each payment method
- [ ] Receive order confirmation
- [ ] View order in admin dashboard
- [ ] Process an order (confirm, ship, deliver)
- [ ] Check mobile view on their own phone

Provide a UAT checklist form. Get written sign-off before launching.

---

### Step 4 — Admin Training

Train the customer on:
- Adding and editing products
- Managing orders
- Customer management
- Discount and offer setup
- Basic report reading
- Handling returns

Provide a short screen recording of each task for future reference.

---

### Step 5 — Go-Live

Before going live:
- DNS pointed to the Atlas domain
- Payment gateway is live (not test mode)
- At least one real test order placed and processed
- Customer knows the process for their first real orders
- Emergency contact confirmed

---

## Atlas Onboarding — Common Problems

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Product data delayed by client | No deadline set | Set a firm "data due date" at the start — no data = no launch |
| Payment gateway issues | Third-party provider delays | Apply for gateway access at Day 1, not Day 10 |
| Design revisions spiral | No sign-off process | One round of revisions included; additional rounds = additional charge |
| Client doesn't know how to manage orders | Training rushed | Record a video of the daily order management workflow |
| Mobile storefront issues | Client only tested on desktop | Always test on mobile explicitly during UAT |

## Critical Rule — Content Gate

**Do not begin design until content is received.**

Missing content causes more project delays than any technical issue.

At kick-off, set explicit deadlines:
> "We need your page content and images by [specific date]. Development begins after content is received. The project timeline starts from that date, not from today."

Get written acknowledgement of this.

---

## Kick-Off Call Agenda

1. Introductions — who is managing this on each side?
2. Confirm scope — review the agreed proposal together
3. Design preferences — references, style, what to avoid
4. Content timeline — who provides what, by when
5. Communication channel — WhatsApp / email / Grekam OS
6. Review cadence — when will we show progress?
7. Approval process — who signs off on design and content?
8. Launch process — hosting, domain, what the client needs to prepare

---

## Design Review Process

Present designs as clickable prototypes (Figma or similar), not static images.

Rules:
- One round of design feedback included
- Feedback must be specific: "Change this colour" not "doesn't feel right"
- Written approval required before development begins
- Changes after development begins may affect timeline and cost

---

## Post-Launch Handover

After go-live, provide:
- Login credentials for all platforms (CMS, hosting, domain)
- A brief "How to update your website" document if they have a CMS
- Instructions for adding new pages or content
- Support contact for post-launch bugs

Bug fix window: [X] days post-launch for bugs. New features are a separate engagement.

## Team Setup Checklist

Before the first session:
- [ ] Collect full team list (name, role, email for each user)
- [ ] Define user roles and permissions (who sees what)
- [ ] Understand their current workflow (what are they doing in Excel / WhatsApp now?)
- [ ] Understand their pipeline stages (custom stages if they differ from default)
- [ ] Understand their lead sources

---

## Training Sessions

### Session 1 — CRM and Leads (60 min)

Cover:
- Creating and managing leads
- Lead stages and pipeline
- Contact and company records
- Logging calls and follow-ups
- Next action rules

End of session task:
> Each team member creates 3 real leads from their current pipeline before Session 2.

---

### Session 2 — Tasks and Projects (45 min)

Cover:
- Creating and assigning tasks
- Setting priorities and deadlines
- Task status management
- Project creation and tracking
- Flagging blockers

End of session task:
> Each team member converts their pending follow-ups into tasks with specific dates.

---

### Session 3 — HR, Reporting, and Manager View (45 min)

Cover:
- Attendance logging
- How management reads the pipeline
- Key reports and what they mean
- Weekly reporting process

End of session:
> Manager reviews the live pipeline together with the onboarding team.

---

## Adoption Risk — The Most Common Failure

**Symptom:** Team attends training, agrees to use the system, then continues with WhatsApp and Excel.

**Why it happens:** No enforcement mechanism. No one checks.

**Prevention:**
1. After Session 1, do a spot check in 48 hours — "Are the 3 leads entered?"
2. After go-live, review the team's activity every Friday for the first month
3. If a team member is not using the system — address it through their manager, not directly
4. Share reports with the client's management so they can see who is and isn't participating

# 30-DAY HEALTH CHECK (All Products)

At Day 30, conduct a structured check-in call with the customer.

## Health Check Questions

| Area | Question |
|---|---|
| **Usage** | Are you and your team using [product] daily? |
| **Value** | What specific result or improvement have you noticed? |
| **Problems** | Is there anything that isn't working the way you expected? |
| **Gaps** | Is there a feature or capability you're not sure how to use? |
| **Team** | Is your whole team onboarded, or is someone still not using it? |
| **Support** | Have you needed support? Was it resolved satisfactorily? |
| **Growth** | Have your needs changed since you started? |

## After the Health Check

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Customer is happy and actively using | Thank them. Ask for a referral. Identify upsell opportunity. |
| Customer is partially using | Identify the gap. Book a follow-up training session. |
| Customer is not using it | Urgent — find out why. May be a churn risk. Escalate to manager. |
| Customer has complaints | Investigate immediately. Do not delay. |
