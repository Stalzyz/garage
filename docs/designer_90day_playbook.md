# 1. THE MOST IMPORTANT PRINCIPLE

## Design is a communication tool, not a personal expression.

Your aesthetic preferences are not the primary input.

The primary inputs are:
1. The business objective (what should the user do on this page?)
2. The brand (who is this company and how do they want to be perceived?)
3. The user (who is looking at this and what do they need to find quickly?)
4. The constraint (what can actually be built by the development team?)

A beautiful design that cannot be built, does not meet the brief, or confuses the user is a failed design.

# 2. DAYS 1–7 — BRAND AND TOOL IMMERSION

## Day 1–3: Brand Study

Before opening Figma for a client:

- [ ] Study every existing Grekam brand asset (logos, colour systems, typography)
- [ ] Study every live product (Grafty, Atlas, Grekam OS, academy website, agency website)
- [ ] Note what is consistent and what is inconsistent across them
- [ ] Identify the visual language: what tone, energy, and values do the designs communicate?

Write a one-paragraph brand summary in your own words. Not a copy of the brief — your interpretation. This will be discussed on Day 5.

## Day 3–5: Tool and File Setup

- [ ] Access to shared Figma workspace confirmed
- [ ] Understand the file naming convention
- [ ] Understand the component library structure
- [ ] Understand where client files live vs. internal files
- [ ] Confirm design export settings and handoff format used with developers

## Day 5–7: Shadow a Project

Before designing anything for a client, shadow one ongoing project.

Watch:
- How briefs are received from the BDM or PM
- How the designer asks clarifying questions before starting
- How options are presented to the client (never one option only)
- How feedback is received and documented
- How the final design is handed off to the developer

Do not offer opinions this week. Only observe and take notes.

# 3. THE DESIGN BRIEF — NEVER SKIP THIS

Before starting any design, you must have a clear brief.

If one is not given to you, ask for it. A design without a brief is guesswork.

**Minimum required before starting:**

- What is this for? (Page, feature, product, marketing asset)
- Who is the target audience?
- What should the user do on this page/screen? (One primary action)
- What are the content elements? (What text, images, data appears here)
- Are there brand guidelines to follow?
- Are there design references the client or PM has shared?
- What are the device requirements? (Mobile-first? Desktop-first? Both?)
- What is the deadline for the first draft?
- Who approves the design? (One person — not "the team")

**If you cannot answer all of the above, ask before you design.**

Time spent designing without a proper brief is time that almost always has to be redone.

---

# 4. THE DESIGN PROCESS

Every client design follows this sequence. Never skip steps.

```
BRIEF RECEIVED
      ↓
CLARIFYING QUESTIONS ASKED
      ↓
CONTENT MAPPED (What content goes where? Before any visual design)
      ↓
WIREFRAME / LOW-FIDELITY (Structure and layout — no colours, no final fonts)
      ↓
WIREFRAME REVIEW (Internal or client — get structural sign-off first)
      ↓
HIGH-FIDELITY DESIGN (Visual design applied to approved structure)
      ↓
DESIGN REVIEW (Internal — quality check before client sees it)
      ↓
CLIENT PRESENTATION (Never send a file — present and explain)
      ↓
FEEDBACK DOCUMENTED
      ↓
REVISIONS (Within agreed scope)
      ↓
DESIGN SIGN-OFF (Written or clear message — not assumed)
      ↓
DEVELOPER HANDOFF
```

**The most common mistake: jumping from brief directly to high-fidelity design.**

This produces beautiful work that solves the wrong problem and requires multiple full reworks.

# 5. PRESENTING TO CLIENTS

You will present design work to clients. This is a skill, not a given.

### Rules for Client Presentations

**Never send a static file without context.**
A Figma link sent on WhatsApp with no explanation is a design death sentence. The client looks at it, doesn't understand the intent, and gives vague or contradictory feedback.

**Always present with a narrative.**
Before showing the design, explain:
> "This page has one job — to get the visitor to click the enquiry button. Everything on this page points toward that. Let me walk you through it."

Then walk through the design top to bottom, explaining the thinking behind each section.

**Present options when there is genuine ambiguity.**
If there are two valid approaches — show both. Label them clearly. Give your recommendation with a reason.

**Never present more than 3 options.**
More than 3 creates decision paralysis, not client empowerment.

**Get specific feedback, not feelings.**
When the client says "I don't like it" — ask:
> "Can you tell me which specific element isn't working for you? Is it the colour, the layout, the imagery, or the overall tone?"

Vague feedback leads to vague revisions.

---

# 6. REVISION MANAGEMENT

Every project has an agreed number of revision rounds. Do not exceed them without a conversation.

### What counts as a revision:
- Changing layout or structure after sign-off
- Replacing images after design delivery
- Changing copy that was client-provided after design delivery
- Adding new sections not in the original brief

### What does not count as a revision:
- Fixing a spacing error
- Correcting an obvious typo in placeholder text
- Adjusting a colour that does not match the brand guide

When a client requests something outside the agreed revision scope:

> "That's a great idea — it's outside the revisions we agreed in the proposal, but I can put together a quick update on the cost and timeline if you'd like to include it. Would that work?"

Do not just do it silently. Scope creep here erodes the project's profitability.

# 7. DEVELOPER HANDOFF — YOUR RESPONSIBILITY DOES NOT END AT DESIGN

A design that cannot be built accurately is an incomplete design.

**Before handoff, verify:**

- [ ] All components are named clearly in Figma
- [ ] Colours use variables/tokens from the design system (not ad-hoc hex codes)
- [ ] Fonts are specified with size, weight, line height, and letter spacing
- [ ] Spacing is consistent and documented (8px grid or equivalent)
- [ ] All states are designed (hover, active, disabled, empty, error, loading)
- [ ] Mobile and desktop breakpoints are both present
- [ ] Images are exported at the correct resolution and format
- [ ] Interactive elements have clear visual indicators (clickable vs. static)
- [ ] Any animations or transitions are described in notes

**After handoff:**
- Be available to answer developer questions
- Review the development implementation before it goes to the client
- Flag discrepancies between your design and the built output — do not accept "close enough" if it affects the user experience

# 8. MONTH 2 — OWN THE CLIENT DESIGN RELATIONSHIP

By Month 2, you should be able to:

- Receive a brief and ask the right clarifying questions without guidance
- Produce wireframes independently
- Present designs to clients in live calls without a senior present
- Document client feedback accurately and act on it
- Complete a developer handoff with all required information
- Identify when a client is requesting out-of-scope work and handle it professionally

**Month 2 Deliverables:**
- 2+ complete client projects handled from brief to handoff
- Zero design reworks due to missed brief requirements
- All design files organised and named correctly in the shared workspace
- Client feedback documented in Grekam OS on each project

---

# 9. MONTH 3 — BUILD SYSTEMS, NOT JUST PAGES

The shift in Month 3: from producing designs to building reusable design infrastructure.

### Month 3 Responsibilities

**Design system contribution:**
- Identify inconsistencies in the existing component library
- Propose and build at least 2 new reusable components
- Document when and how to use them

**Template creation:**
- Build at least one reusable page template for a common project type (e.g., standard business homepage, standard product page)
- This template should allow the next project to start 30% faster

**Quality review process:**
- Establish a personal quality checklist (beyond what is in this document) for your own design type
- Review a colleague's design using the checklist and give constructive written feedback

---

# 10. YOUR OPERATING RULES

Ten rules for how we design at Grekam:

1. **Understand the brief before opening Figma.**
2. **Design for the user, not for yourself.**
3. **Content before design — structure before visuals.**
4. **Never present a design without a narrative.**
5. **Get structural sign-off before investing in high-fidelity.**
6. **Document every piece of client feedback, every time.**
7. **Know what can be built before you design it.**
8. **Revisions outside scope are a commercial conversation, not free work.**
9. **A developer handoff is part of your job, not someone else's problem.**
10. **Build components, not just screens. Think in systems.**
