# Grekam — Objection Handling Handbook
### Version 1.0 | Sales Team Reference | August 2026

---

> An objection is not a rejection.
> It is a question in disguise.
> Your job is to find the real question and answer it.

---

# HOW TO USE THIS HANDBOOK

1. **Listen to the full objection.** Do not interrupt.
2. **Acknowledge it.** Never dismiss or minimise.
3. **Understand what's underneath it.** Most objections mask a deeper concern.
4. **Respond with one of the options below.** Choose the most natural for the conversation.
5. **Ask a follow-up question.** Every response should end with a question that re-opens the conversation.
6. **Do not argue.** Ever.

---

# CATEGORY 1 — PRICE OBJECTIONS

---

## Objection 1.1 — "It's too expensive."

**What's underneath it:**
They may not see the value clearly enough, or they are comparing to a cheaper alternative without understanding the difference.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "I understand. Can I ask — when you say expensive, what are you comparing it to?"
> *(Listen. Then address the specific comparison.)*

> Option B:
> "That's a fair concern. Let me ask you — how much is your team currently spending [in time / tool cost / missed opportunities] on managing this manually?"
> *(Reframe cost as cost of not solving the problem)*

> Option C:
> "Expensive compared to what you expected, or expensive in absolute terms? Because the two are different conversations and I want to make sure I address the right one."

**Follow-up question:**
> "If the investment were not a concern, would you feel confident moving forward?"
*(If yes — the only issue is price. Work on value or package.)*

---

## Objection 1.2 — "Can you give me a discount?"

**What's underneath it:**
Testing your confidence in the product's value. Rarely a genuine budget crisis on the first ask.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "I appreciate you asking directly. Before I discuss pricing flexibility, help me understand — is the current price a real budget constraint, or is it more that you want to make sure you're getting the best deal?"

> Option B:
> "Our pricing is structured around the value we deliver. Let me make sure the package I've proposed is the right one for your situation first — sometimes there's a more suitable option that also makes the investment more comfortable."

> Option C:
> "I can't discount without approval, but what I can do is make sure you're on the right plan so you're not paying for things you don't need. Let's go through it together."

**What you never do:**
Offer a discount immediately. This signals that the original price was inflated and damages the relationship long-term.

---

## Objection 1.3 — "We don't have the budget right now."

**What's underneath it:**
Could be genuine. Could be a polite deflection. Explore before accepting.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "I completely understand budget constraints. Can I ask — is this a matter of the budget not existing, or has it been allocated to something else right now?"
> *(Genuine budget = discuss timeline. Allocated elsewhere = help them see priority.)*

> Option B:
> "Would it help if we started with a smaller scope and expanded once you've seen the results? Some of our clients prefer to begin with [entry package] and grow from there."

> Option C:
> "When do you expect the budget situation to change? I'd rather follow up at the right time than push now."

# CATEGORY 2 — TIMING OBJECTIONS

---

## Objection 2.1 — "I'll think about it."

**What's underneath it:**
They are not ready to decide. There is an unspoken concern they haven't shared.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "Of course. Is there something specific you're working through, or is it more that you need a bit more time to evaluate?"
> *(Find the real concern)*

> Option B:
> "Absolutely — I wouldn't want you to rush a decision. What information would help you feel more confident about it?"

> Option C:
> "What's the one thing that would make this a clear yes for you?"

**Follow-up:**
Set a specific follow-up date before ending the call.
> "When would be a good time for me to check back — in two or three days?"

---

## Objection 2.2 — "This isn't the right time."

**What's underneath it:**
Genuine timing issue, or a way of saying the priority isn't there.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "I understand. What does the right time look like for you? I want to follow up when it actually makes sense."

> Option B:
> "Can I ask what's happening in the next few months that makes this feel premature? Understanding that helps me know how to support you better."

> Option C:
> "Some of our clients said the same thing and then found that starting sooner gave them a real advantage by the time things picked up. But I respect your timeline — when should I reconnect?"

---

## Objection 2.3 — "Send me details and I'll get back to you."

**What's underneath it:**
A way of ending the call without committing. Often means they're not yet convinced enough to continue talking.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "Happy to send that across. What specific part would be most useful for me to include so I send you what's actually relevant?"
> *(This re-engages the conversation and shows you're not just going to blast a brochure)*

> Option B:
> "Of course. And rather than sending a generic overview, can I send you something specific to your situation? It'll be more useful than a standard brochure."

> Option C:
> "I'll send that now. Can we book a 10-minute call for [specific day] to go through your questions after you've had a look?"

# CATEGORY 3 — COMPETITOR OBJECTIONS

---

## Objection 3.1 — "We're already using [Competitor]."

**What's underneath it:**
They may be happy, or they may have complaints they haven't voiced.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "That's great — it means you already understand the value of this kind of solution. Can I ask — what are you happy with about it, and where do you feel it falls short?"
> *(Never attack the competitor. Let them tell you the gaps.)*

> Option B:
> "Interesting. How long have you been using them? And what made you pick them over the alternatives at the time?"

> Option C:
> "Some of our current clients came from [Competitor]. The reasons were usually [specific known differentiator]. I'm not saying they're wrong for you — but it might be worth a 15-minute comparison. Would that be useful?"

**Important:**
Never say a competitor is bad. You don't know their situation. Instead, ask questions that surface the gap between what the competitor offers and what the customer actually needs.

---

## Objection 3.2 — "I found a cheaper option."

**What's underneath it:**
They're comparing price without fully comparing value. Or the cheaper option is genuinely sufficient.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "That's worth exploring. Can I ask — what does the cheaper option include? I want to make sure you're comparing fairly, because sometimes the difference is in things that matter a lot over time."

> Option B:
> "Cheaper upfront sometimes ends up costing more in support, limitations, or migration later. What's most important to you — the initial price or the long-term fit?"

> Option C:
> "If the only difference were price, the cheaper option would always win. But our clients choose us for [specific reason — support / Indian context / customisation / integration]. Is any of that relevant to your decision?"

# CATEGORY 4 — TRUST AND CREDIBILITY OBJECTIONS

---

## Objection 4.1 — "We've never heard of you."

**What's underneath it:**
Risk concern. They're worried about committing to an unknown company.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "That's completely fair — and an honest thing to raise. We're a growing company, which means our clients get direct attention rather than being a ticket number. Can I show you what some of our existing clients have built with us?"

> Option B:
> "We work with businesses in [industry they're in] — would it help if I shared a relevant case or put you in touch with a client who started in a similar situation?"

> Option C:
> "Being newer means we're hungrier to prove ourselves. Our team is directly accessible — you'd work with us, not a support queue. Does that change the picture at all?"

---

## Objection 4.2 — "Can you show me examples of your work?"

**This is not an objection. It is a buying signal.**

Response:

> "Absolutely — let me share [specific relevant example]. This client was in a similar situation to yours. What they needed was [X], and here's what we built."

Tailor the example to their industry or problem. Do not show a generic portfolio dump.

---

## Objection 4.3 — "What happens if something goes wrong?"

**What's underneath it:**
Risk management. They want assurance before committing.

**Response Options:**

> Option A:
> "Great question — let me walk you through our support process. [Explain support channel, response time, escalation path]. Is there a specific scenario you're thinking about?"

> Option B:
> "We've handled [specific type of issue] before — here's how it typically works. Our goal is that you always have a person you can reach, not just a form to fill in."

# CATEGORY 5 — PRODUCT-SPECIFIC OBJECTIONS

---

## GRAFTY OBJECTIONS

**"Does this work with WhatsApp without getting banned?"**

> "Yes — Grafty operates on the official WhatsApp Business API, which is approved by Meta. It's the same infrastructure that banks and large businesses use. You're not using a third-party workaround — it's the official channel."

**"We already use WhatsApp Business app — why do we need this?"**

> "The WhatsApp Business app is a great starting point. But it has limits — only one user at a time, no automation, no broadcast to over 256 contacts, no analytics. Grafty gives you everything the app doesn't. When your volume grows, the app becomes a bottleneck."

**"Can our whole team use it?"**

> "Yes — multiple users, different roles, and you can control who sees what. That's actually one of the main reasons businesses move to Grafty — so more than one person can manage customer conversations."

---

## ATLAS OBJECTIONS

**"Why not just use Shopify?"**

> "Shopify is a great product — globally. Atlas is built for the Indian market — UPI, COD, local payment gateways, GST-ready invoicing, and hosted locally. For Indian businesses, the setup and ongoing experience is usually smoother and more cost-effective with Atlas."

**"Can it handle [specific feature]?"**

> "Let me be honest with you — if it's a standard ecommerce feature, almost certainly yes. If it's something highly custom, I'll check with our team before committing. I'd rather be accurate than overpromise. Can you tell me more about exactly what you need?"

**"What if we outgrow it?"**

> "Atlas is built to scale. Our largest clients manage thousands of orders and hundreds of products. If you share your growth projections, I can tell you where you'd sit in 12 to 24 months and whether any customisation would be needed."

---

## WEB DEVELOPMENT OBJECTIONS

**"How long will it take?"**

> "It depends on scope — a standard business website takes 3 to 4 weeks. An ecommerce build is typically 6 to 10 weeks. A custom application can vary significantly. Once we scope the project together, I can give you a realistic timeline. Can we spend 20 minutes on that?"

**"We had a bad experience with another agency."**

> "I'm sorry to hear that — it's more common than it should be. Can I ask what went wrong? Understanding that helps me address it directly rather than making the same mistake."
> *(Listen carefully — this tells you exactly what they need to see from you.)*

**"Can you match this other agency's price?"**

> "I'd want to understand what they're including before I answer that. Sometimes the gap in price reflects a gap in scope, quality, or ongoing support. What exactly are they offering for that price?"

# UNIVERSAL CLOSING MOVE

After handling any objection, always re-qualify the conversation:

> "Does that address your concern?"

*(If yes)* → Move toward the next step:
> "So based on that, does it make sense to move forward?"

*(If no)* → Go deeper:
> "What else would you need to feel comfortable?"

---

# WHAT TO DO WHEN YOU DON'T KNOW THE ANSWER

Never guess. Never make something up to avoid losing the momentum.

Say:

> "That's a specific question — I want to make sure I give you the right answer rather than guess. Let me confirm with our team and come back to you by [specific time]. Does that work?"

Then follow through. On time. With the answer.

**Honesty, especially on hard questions, builds more trust than a confident wrong answer.**
