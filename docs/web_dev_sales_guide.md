# Web Development — Sales Guide
### For Non-Technical Salespeople | August 2026

---

> You do not need to write code to sell web projects well.
> You need to understand what is being built, what it costs to build,
> and how to protect the company from promises that cannot be delivered.

---

# SECTION 1 — The Classification System

Before you can quote, scope, or pitch — you need to classify the project correctly.

Use this reference every time a customer says "I need a website."

---

## Type 1 — Static / Brochure Website

**What it is:** A presentational website. No login. No purchases. No user data. Just information.

**Typical pages:** Home, About, Services, Portfolio, Contact

**Who needs it:** Local businesses, professionals, consultants, startups establishing credibility

**Complexity:** Low

**Example sites:** Clinic website, law firm, interior designer, freelancer portfolio

**What to ask:**
- How many pages?
- Do you need a blog?
- Do you have content (text and images) ready?
- Do you need a contact form?

---

## Type 2 — Business Website with Lead Generation

**What it is:** A website designed to convert visitors into enquiries. Forms, CTAs, booking systems, WhatsApp integration.

**Typical pages:** Home, Services (multiple), About, Testimonials, Contact, Booking/Enquiry

**Who needs it:** Service businesses, agencies, clinics, training centres, event companies

**Complexity:** Low to medium

**Additional elements:**
- Multi-step enquiry forms
- WhatsApp chat button integration
- SEO-optimised structure
- Google Analytics / Meta Pixel integration

---

## Type 3 — Ecommerce Website

**What it is:** A store where customers can browse, add to cart, and complete a purchase online.

**Who needs it:** Retail businesses, D2C brands, wholesalers going online

**Complexity:** Medium to high

**Our product:** Atlas (always recommend Atlas for this)

**What distinguishes it:** Payment integration, inventory, order management, customer accounts

---

## Type 4 — Web Application / SaaS

**What it is:** A software product that users log into and interact with. Has user accounts, data, workflows, and business logic.

**Examples:** Booking management system, customer portal, internal tool, inventory system, HR platform, CRM

**Who needs it:** Businesses with complex internal processes, SaaS startups, enterprises

**Complexity:** High to very high

**Important:** Always escalate to management before scoping or quoting a web application. The requirements gathering alone requires a dedicated session.

---

## Type 5 — Custom Integrations and Add-ons

**What it is:** Adding specific functionality to an existing system. Not a full new build.

**Examples:**
- WhatsApp integration into an existing website
- Payment gateway integration
- CRM plugin for a current website
- Third-party API connection (courier, ERP, payment, analytics)

**Complexity:** Varies significantly by integration

**Rule:** Never quote integrations without technical review first. Always say: "Let me confirm the technical scope and come back to you."

# SECTION 2 — How to Scope a Web Project

The most common mistake in web sales is assuming you understand the scope before you ask questions.

A website can be ₹15,000 or ₹5,00,000. The difference is entirely in the requirements.

### The Scoping Conversation

Ask these in every web sales conversation:

**About the project:**
- What is the main purpose of this website?
- Who is the audience? (consumers / businesses / both)
- What do you want visitors to do when they land on it?
- Do you have an existing website? What are you unhappy with?

**About the content:**
- Who will write the page content? (Client / Grekam)
- Do you have photos, videos, or product images?
- Do you have a logo and brand colours?
- Do you have design references — websites you like?

**About the functionality:**
- Do users need to log in or create accounts?
- Do you need to take payments?
- Do you need a booking or appointment system?
- Do you need a contact form or enquiry form?
- Do you need to connect to any other system? (WhatsApp, CRM, payment, delivery)
- Do you need a blog or news section?
- Do you need multi-language support?
- Does the client need to update content themselves? (CMS requirement)

**About the project:**
- What is the timeline?
- Is there a specific launch event or date this is tied to?
- Who approves designs on the client side?
- Who is the single point of contact?
- What is the budget range?

# SECTION 3 — Complexity Estimator

Use this to guide your internal estimate before getting a formal quote from the team.

| Factor | Low Complexity | Medium Complexity | High Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number of pages | 1–5 | 6–15 | 16+ |
| Custom design | Template | Modified template | Fully custom |
| User login / accounts | No | Optional | Yes |
| Payment integration | No | Standard gateway | Multiple / custom |
| Third-party integrations | None | 1–2 standard | Multiple / custom API |
| CMS (client edits content) | No | Simple blog/pages | Full CMS |
| Multilingual | No | No | Yes |
| Content provided by | Client provides all | Mixed | Grekam creates content |
| Special animations / interactions | None | Standard | Complex / custom |

**How to use this:**
- All Low → Simple project → Fast quote
- Mix of Low and Medium → Standard project → Team review needed
- Any High → Complex project → Always involves a proper scoping session before quoting

# SECTION 4 — What to Promise. What Never to Promise.

This is the most important rule in web sales.

---

### You CAN say:

> "We build websites that are mobile-optimised, fast, and designed for your business."

> "We will check whether [specific feature] is possible and confirm before we commit."

> "Our standard ecommerce build takes 6 to 8 weeks. That can vary depending on the scope."

> "That integration is technically possible — let me confirm the effort and cost with our team."

---

### You MUST NEVER say:

> "Yes, we can definitely do that." *(without technical confirmation for anything non-standard)*

> "It'll be done in two weeks." *(without the development team confirming the timeline)*

> "We'll build whatever you need." *(open-ended scope)*

> "That's very simple — shouldn't take long." *(timeline assessments without developer input)*

> "This can easily connect to your [ERP / accounting system / third-party tool]." *(integrations always need technical confirmation)*

---

### When you don't know, say this:

> "That's a specific technical requirement. Let me confirm the details with our team and come back to you with a clear answer by [specific time]. I'd rather give you the right answer than guess."

This builds more trust than a confident wrong answer.

# SECTION 5 — The Development Process (What to Explain to Clients)

Every client wants to know what happens after they pay. Have this explanation ready.

```
PHASE 1 — DISCOVERY & BRIEF
We collect your requirements, content, and design preferences.
[Duration: 2–5 days]

PHASE 2 — DESIGN
We create the visual design (wireframes first, then full designs).
You review and approve before we write a single line of code.
[Duration: 5–10 days]

PHASE 3 — DEVELOPMENT
We build the approved design into a live, functional website.
[Duration: 10–20 days depending on scope]

PHASE 4 — REVIEW & TESTING
You test the website on our staging environment.
We fix bugs and incorporate feedback.
[Duration: 3–5 days]

PHASE 5 — LAUNCH
We go live on your domain.
[Duration: 1–2 days]

PHASE 6 — POST-LAUNCH SUPPORT
Bug fixes included for [X] days post-launch.
[Ongoing: Optional maintenance retainer available]
```

**Key point to emphasise:**
> "The timeline begins from when we receive your content and design approval — not from the date of payment. The most common cause of delays is content not being ready."

# SECTION 6 — Pricing Framework

Do not quote specific prices from memory. Always refer to the current pricing document or confirm with the BDM.

However, you need to understand the general pricing framework to guide conversations.

**Pricing factors:**
- Number of pages and complexity
- Custom design vs. template
- Functionality (e-commerce, bookings, logins, integrations)
- Content creation (who provides it)
- CMS (can the client edit it after launch)
- Timeline (rush projects may carry a premium)
- Maintenance and support post-launch

**How to handle the pricing question in the first call:**

> "Our web projects typically range from ₹[X] for a standard business website to ₹[X+] for a full ecommerce build or custom application. Once I understand your exact requirements, I can give you a precise figure. Can you tell me more about what you need?"

Never give a final price in the first call without a proper scope.

# SECTION 7 — How to Brief the Development Team

After a project is sold, your job is to give the development team everything they need to begin — without them ever calling you for clarification.

**A complete project brief includes:**

1. What type of website (from Section 1 above)
2. Number of pages (listed individually)
3. Design references (links or screenshots)
4. Logo and brand assets (or note that they are pending)
5. Content — who provides, deadline, current status
6. All functionality requirements (forms, payments, integrations, CMS)
7. What the client specifically asked for
8. What the client specifically does NOT want
9. Delivery deadline
10. Client's point of contact for design approvals

Refer to the **Sales-to-Development Handoff SOP** for the complete form.

# SECTION 8 — Common Client Phrases and What They Actually Mean

| Client Says | What It Usually Means | Your Action |
|---|---|---|
| "Just a simple website" | They have not thought about the scope | Ask all scoping questions before agreeing to "simple" |
| "Something like [big brand website]" | They want high-end design at low-end price | Explore which specific elements they like, then scope realistically |
| "We need it next week" | They are anxious about timing | Explain the process; be honest about what's realistic |
| "We want full ownership of the code" | They've had a bad experience with agencies holding them hostage | Clarify your delivery terms upfront |
| "Can you just copy this website?" | They think copying is fast and easy | Explain that "copying" still requires full development; scope accordingly |
| "We'll send content later" | Content will arrive late, causing delays | Set a firm content deadline at project start |

# SECTION 9 — Web Development Certification

**Level 1 — Classifier:**
- Correctly classify any described website into its type (1–5)
- Know which complexity factors apply

**Level 2 — Qualifier:**
- Run a full scoping conversation using the Section 2 questions
- Produce a scope summary a developer can read and understand

**Level 3 — Salesperson:**
- Explain the full development process to a client confidently
- Know what to promise and what to defer
- Handle the 5 most common web sales objections

**Level 4 — Consultant:**
- Identify which type of web project a client needs (even when they describe it incorrectly)
- Flag custom requirements before committing
- Write a complete project brief for the development team
